\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The later-session evaluation changes the aggregate incremental direction relative to development, but the improvement is concentrated in D11 rather than replicated uniformly across D11--D13. Under the frozen pooled-and-both-family criterion, the primary 20-check comparison is favorable in aggregate, while historical benefit remains heterogeneous and the secondary envelope-schedule superiority criterion is unsupported. FIS improves relative to affine B0 but remains worse than unchanged consensus in MAE. These distinctions reinforce the need to report historical, incremental, family and session effects separately.

\subsection{Control initialization before assigning reference value}
Historical correction already improves pooled RF performance before a new reference is bought in both stages; confirmation additionally supports an incremental aggregate benefit. The reporting identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp} exposes this distinction separately for MAE and exceedance. Retaining family results prevents a pooled improvement from concealing adverse FIS MAE. Additional-reference and schedule comparisons must use identical exposure keys and an effective same-estimator B0.

\subsection{Update information and conditional sensitivity}
Cumulative RLS can absorb new references: its gain is nonzero for informative regressors. The response depends on leverage, innovation and historical information, not merely 100 versus 20 rows. Forgetting at purchases discounts old information and ridge; it does not forget during unqueried exposures. The fixed $.95$ comparator and $.90$ sensitivity, including one native schedule, increase the scope of the comparison without supporting a claim that any tested method is optimal. Actual state changes and leverage are archived.

In development, every pooled RF B20 affine MAE point change is adverse, while some lower-budget pooled point changes are favorable and endpoint and family patterns vary. OLS/ridge provide some small favorable incremental changes, showing dependence on upstream prediction and mask. Conditional resampling bounds describe the frozen development paths; crossing zero does not prove absence or equivalence, and excluding zero does not confer population significance after sequential development. Quarter-path, lag-one and trend diagnostics expose temporal structure rather than assume away nonstationarity.

\subsection{Prediction sets and measurement assurance}
Conditional minimax midpoint behavior is distinct from empirical MAE. Projection's non-worsening property requires the actual residual to lie within the assumed interval, and hull containment cannot rescue a contradictory intersection. Frequent containment, a narrow radius and complete-array service assurance are therefore separate properties. Confirmation contains both noncontainment and false-assurance failures, with the latter concentrated in D11. These midpoint-envelope diagnostics should not be interpreted as uncertainty intervals for the affine estimator. Standard conformal methods provide a useful future comparison only under their own exchangeability or sequential assumptions \cite{angelopoulos2021}; neither descriptive envelopes nor ordinary split conformal offer unconditional protection against arbitrary drift.

Traceable co-calibration requires characterized references and uncertainty propagation \cite{gruber2023,jcgm100,jcgm200}. Multiple accesses to one generated-concentration system cannot identify a shared reference bias. The present analysis is therefore a reference-relative algorithm comparison. The fixed-D10 temporal confirmation supplies distinct later-session evidence, but same-apparatus confirmation and session-specific reversals limit how far its aggregate direction can be generalized.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
Both stages use one chamber and array: three development assessment sessions and three confirmation sessions, each with twelve overlapping peer configurations. Sequential extensions were informed by earlier development results; family emphasis, forgetting, native scheduling and conditional resampling are declared extensions rather than independent preregistration. Error is relative to released nominal CO with stated relative uncertainty below 5.5\%, without exposure-level reference-error covariance or hard bounds. Independent-array coverage and a causal drift diagnosis are not established. Acquisition latency and cost are simulated, and resets limit the horizon of late purchases. R7/R14 and the UCI270 reserved batches remain unused; D11--D13 are evaluated only in the fixed-D10 temporal confirmation.

The calibrated hardware union includes units omitted from individual peer configurations; strict unseen-unit or single-sensor deployment is a different estimand. Late-core summaries omit transient and washout behavior, and anonymous positions do not establish serial/lot provenance. Inverse regression and numerical rank do not identify forward physical parameters. Empirical slopes and fixed temporal/session allowances are unvalidated future bounds. Ordinary block resampling requires regularity that these adaptive chronological paths may not satisfy; the saved development intervals neither rerun adaptive trajectories nor propagate fitting or reference uncertainty. The finite comparison does not rank all possible online methods.

\section{Conclusion}
Historical and incremental reference effects must be separated when evaluating reference-assisted calibration. In development, historical affine correction provides most of the observed affine gain before any additional reference is purchased, and all fifteen pooled B20 RF affine MAE changes are adverse. In the later-session evaluation, the frozen aggregate incremental criterion is met, but the improvement is dominated by D11; D12 worsens slightly and D13 changes little. Historical benefit remains heterogeneous, FIS remains worse than unchanged consensus after the primary incremental update, and schedule superiority is not established.

The interval analysis reaches a different conclusion from the point-error comparison. False midpoint assurances and interval failures persist in the later sessions, showing that improved aggregate MAE does not imply reliable assurance. The contribution is therefore an information-matched chronological evaluation of established calibration rules, with explicit historical baselines, incremental contrasts, family/session reporting, causal reference ordering and reference cost. The evidence does not establish a new calibration algorithm, traceable measurement uncertainty or independent-array generalization.

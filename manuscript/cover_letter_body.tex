\noindent Please consider our manuscript ``\ManuscriptTitle'' for publication in \emph{Measurement}. The study examines a practical measurement-methodology question: how much apparent improvement from reference-assisted calibration is attributable to historical recalibration, and how much is attributable to newly acquired reference checks?

Using public temperature-modulated CO sensor measurements, we separate historical correction, incremental reference access and schedule effects under prediction-before-reference ordering. The study includes chronological development and a frozen fixed-D10 evaluation on three later sessions of the same apparatus. Under the prespecified aggregate criterion, the primary 20-check affine comparison is favorable in confirmation, but the improvement is dominated by D11 rather than reproduced uniformly across D11--D13. Historical benefit remains heterogeneous, and FIS improves relative to affine B0 while remaining worse than unchanged consensus in MAE.

The manuscript also reports unfavorable evidence rather than selecting a post hoc winner. Session reversals preclude a uniform-improvement claim, envelope-schedule superiority is unsupported, and midpoint-envelope diagnostics contain substantial false assurance in the later-session evaluation. These results distinguish point-error improvement from measurement assurance and motivate explicit reporting of each estimator's zero-check baseline, family/session effects and reference cost.

The correction algorithms themselves are established methods. The contribution is the information-matched chronological evaluation, causal reference-access accounting, temporally separated assessment and transparent retention of contradictory results. The original measurements are publicly available from UCI, and the code, frozen numerical evidence and reproduction scripts are deposited at \url{https://github.com/asayghe1/CO-Sensor-Reference-Calibration}.

The study evaluates historical correction and the incremental value of additional reference checks in a public CO sensor-array replay, using established calibration algorithms. It includes chronological development and a completed, separately frozen fixed-D10 temporal confirmation on three later sessions of the same apparatus.

The frozen descriptive benefit criterion requires lower strict 1 ppm exceedance and nonpositive mean absolute error (MAE) change pooled and in both sensor families. The prespecified affine envelope-driven 20-check confirmation comparison meets the incremental aggregate criterion: pooled MAE changes from 1.3553 to 1.3257 ppm and strict exceedance from 43.06\% to 42.14\%. This reverses the adverse development B20 MAE pattern. Historical benefit remains heterogeneous; FIS improves from affine B0 at 0.9471 to 0.9033 ppm but remains worse than unchanged consensus at 0.8820 ppm. Session reversals preclude uniform improvement, and envelope-schedule superiority is unsupported.

The manuscript retains unfavorable results and separates interval noncontainment from midpoint false assurance: 38 false assurances among 74 flagged outputs and among 3,600 total confirmation outputs. These are midpoint-envelope diagnostics, not affine uncertainty intervals. Conditional resampling is development-only; no confirmation bootstrap was performed. No new calibration algorithm, certified uncertainty or independent-array validation is claimed.

The original measurements are public through UCI. Complete development and temporal-confirmation evidence is retained in the existing local reproducibility archive; the final code/data-access and licensing arrangement awaits author confirmation.
